What should you do with that?

Read posts that beat an author's usual result. Look at the topic, opening and proof. Then ask which idea fits the work you can actually show. There is no fixed hook length or ending that wins for every post.

How the studies were done and what they can tell us +

The October 9 writing study looked at 10,629 public LinkedIn posts from 140 authors in three niches. It compared engagement relative to each author's own baseline. Openings based on work the author did or saw and bodies that carried a story had positive associations in parts of that sample. Niches differed, and the work-only follow-up weakened broad advice to add personal moments.

A separate October 16 hook study used 17,322 posts from four research cohorts and selected same-author extremes. A preview had some signal in those peer comparisons but did not reliably rank Ivan's own posts. Neither study proves that a wording change causes more engagement. Likes and reposts also cannot tell us whether a buyer became a lead.
