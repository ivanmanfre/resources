1. Agreement and start

This attribution revision replaces the earlier proposed versions dated 11 and 13 October 2026 and takes effect only when both parties sign this revised version. Each signer confirms authority to bind the named party. “Provider” and “Client” refer to the parties above. Days are calendar days unless stated otherwise; business days exclude weekends and public holidays in Italy.

The service starts on the date of the first successful subscription payment after both signatures. Client should pay when ready to provide access and begin onboarding. Each service period lasts 30 days from that date. No work or reserved start date is required before payment. If payment is taken before both signatures, Provider promptly refunds it and stops premature recurring billing. Client restarts checkout after both signatures; early payment does not start the service or minimum term. If payment has not been made within 30 days of the last signature, either party may end the unstarted agreement by email without a service fee.

2. Included service

Provider runs acquisition activity for Jonathan Kiekbusch’s LinkedIn account: strategy and positioning; five posts per week, normally two reach, two mid-funnel and one buyer post; research, writing, design and publishing; relevant resources and X articles, with newsletters as the subscriber list develops.

Outreach focuses on businesses based in the United States and Canada, subject to section 4.2. Included outreach covers relevant contacts and engagers, connection invitations, DMs and available InMail credits, reply handling, qualification, booking, pre-call preparation and post-call follow-up for service-sourced prospects. A client panel, Slack feedback and a short monthly review are included.

The monthly plan sets resource priorities and publishing dates. One consolidated revision round per post or resource is included. Additional accounts, unrelated development, paid advertising, cold email, management of pre-existing pipeline, additional revision rounds and third-party costs require written scope and price agreement before work begins.

3. Base fee and payment

Client authorises a $1,000 recurring subscription every 30 days. The minimum service commitment is three periods. Commission is invoiced separately. Fees exclude taxes legally chargeable and separately shown on an invoice. Each party bears its own income taxes; withholding required by law must be documented.

Earned fees and started periods are non-refundable except for Provider’s uncured material breach or another express right in this agreement. Cancelling the Stripe subscription does not itself terminate the agreement or remove fees properly due. Client pays undisputed amounts when due.

Which business earns commission

4. Attributed prospects

4.1 Source and timing. An attributed prospect is a non-excluded business whose first sales inquiry or booking for the opportunity qualifies under (a) or (b) below. Use the earlier inquiry or booking time. A substantive service-related reply counts as an inquiry; rescheduling or a repeat booking does not restart the test. Section 4.2 applies to every route.

(a) Direct evidence. Identifiable dated records must establish that Provider’s work generated the inquiry: a substantive sales reply to Provider’s outreach, a service-related resource inquiry, a tracked campaign-to-inquiry path, or a written/recorded prospect statement identifying Provider’s work as the source. The generating outreach, content or resource must have been delivered during the service. A later source statement may establish that earlier origin. The seven-day activity limit below does not apply to this direct-evidence route; section 6 still limits post-termination eligibility.

(b) Seven-day activity rule. A prospect is presumed attributable when the same identified person visited the managed LinkedIn profile, or reacted, commented or reposted content produced under this engagement, during the service and within the 168 hours before their first inquiry or booking, and the completed source check in (c) finds no credible competing source. Match the LinkedIn identity using verified email or company details; a name alone is insufficient. A colleague’s activity needs a recorded introduction or source statement connecting it to the same opportunity. Activity after the inquiry cannot qualify. Estimated activity times qualify only if the whole possible time range falls inside the window; post publication and data-import dates do not establish interaction timing. Later reliable contrary evidence may rebut the presumption.

(c) Other-channel check. Client identifies its active acquisition channels before launch and supplies relevant person/opportunity records through the dedicated pipeline or redacted exports. Check original and latest source history and dates, source answers, prior inquiries, referrals, campaign conversations and identifiable paid clicks/forms before the first inquiry or booking. The log records what was checked and any unavailable records. A blank or “Direct” source field alone does not complete the check. Anonymous views, impressions, aggregate ad-conversion claims, unanswered outbound messages and unsupported source labels alone establish neither party’s source.

(d) Competing sources. An established independent origin defeats attribution. Where only profile activity or engagement supports Provider and an identifiable competing source also precedes the inquiry, review the case under 4.3. This includes profile view, then person-linked paid-ad click, then booking, even within seven days. Once direct evidence establishes Provider generated the first inquiry, a later website visit, brand search, retargeting click or ordinary follow-up alone does not remove attribution. Credible contrary origin evidence remains reviewable. Uncertain identity/timing, incomplete checks and conflicting evidence stay pending; disputed commission requires written allocation or resolution under section 13. No automatic split applies.

4.2 Exclusions and baseline. Chinese businesses, including their US and other international subsidiaries and brands, are outside scope. This means businesses incorporated or headquartered in China and entities or brands they control. Exclusions also cover all past and current SwishDM clients, businesses recorded in Client’s CRM or ClickUp during the 24 months before the service starts, and prospects independently sourced through Client’s partner referrals, conferences or events.

Before outreach starts, Client supplies a dated baseline identifying excluded companies, domains and relevant parent/subsidiary or brand relationships. Client promptly records later independent referrals or event contacts. An omission may be corrected using contemporaneous evidence of an earlier relationship; a later referral, event meeting or CRM entry does not retrospectively exclude a prospect already sourced by Provider. Work on excluded accounts requires a separate written scope and commission agreement.

4.3 Shared booking and record. A shared booking link may be used on Client’s website and LinkedIn. Client automatically copies all new sales bookings, cancellations and reschedules into the accessible dedicated pipeline. The link alone does not establish source. Provider keeps a shared log of identity, first inquiry/booking, source dates, evidence and check status. Client supplies relevant missing records or identifies what is unavailable; withholding required records is subject to 8.2. Either party should raise disputes within ten business days of a monthly report, identifying the conflicting evidence or exclusion. Silence does not conclusively establish attribution; an omitted log entry does not defeat reliable evidence. Disputes follow section 13 without delaying unrelated payments.

Commission and closing window · 3 / 7

Commission and later closes

5. Rate and revenue base

5.1 Rate. Client pays 15% of revenue actually collected for each attributed client’s first three consecutive monthly service-retainer periods, including retainer upgrades within those periods. The first period begins when that client’s paid retainer service begins. A different billing frequency does not extend the three-month limit.

5.2 Calculation. Exclude VAT/sales tax, separately identified advertising spend, genuine third-party pass-through costs, refunds and chargebacks. Client’s own labour, overhead and payment-processing charges are not deductions. Allocate advance, annual or bundled payments reasonably to the services and periods they cover using the customer contract; do not charge commission twice. Standalone one-off projects require a separate commission agreement. Relabelling a recurring retainer does not change its substance.

5.3 Collection. Commission becomes payable when eligible revenue is collected, even if collected after the service ends or a reporting window closes. Revenue in other currencies is converted to USD using the documented settlement rate or, if unavailable, the ECB reference cross-rate for the receipt date. Genuine refunds reduce commission through the next invoice or repayment within 30 days if no invoice remains.

6. Business closing after termination

6.1 Existing pipeline. Prospects attributed during the service remain eligible if they enter a binding customer contract or make their first payment within 90 days after the service ends.

6.2 Later inbound. A new inquiry first received within 90 days after the service ends qualifies under the direct-evidence route in 4.1(a), or the seven-day rule in 4.1(b) using a qualifying interaction during the service. The source checks, competing-source rules and exclusions in section 4 apply. The prospect must contract or first pay within the same 90-day closing window. General brand awareness or unrelated future inbound is excluded.

6.3 Boundaries. The rate applicable to that prospect under section 9 and the first-three-month limit apply. There is no continuing base fee after termination and no perpetual commission on the audience. Within ten business days after termination, the parties exchange the pipeline snapshot and links to relevant delivered assets. The snapshot supports evidence and is not the sole condition of entitlement.

Reporting, renewal and exit · 4 / 7

Payment and a clear exit

7. Reporting, visibility and verification

7.1 Monthly reports. Within ten business days after each calendar month, Client reports covered inquiries, source evidence, deal status, contract dates, eligible invoices, collections, refunds and unpaid balances, including nil reports. Reports continue through the section 6 windows and until eligible revenue is collected or otherwise evidenced as finally reconciled. Commission invoices supported by evidence are payable within seven days; a specific dispute does not delay the undisputed balance.

7.2 Dedicated pipeline. Client provides access to a dedicated sales pipeline in Close or a separate ClickUp space for covered prospects. No access to Client’s main ClickUp workspace or unrelated client information is required. The pipeline records source, deal status, contract date/value and eligible collections; Client supplies relevant contract, invoice and payment evidence on request.

After termination, read-only pipeline access continues through the 90-day closing window in section 6. After that, Client may end pipeline access and provide equivalent monthly reports or secure exports solely for eligible deals with outstanding collections or commission. Those reports and supporting evidence continue until the eligible revenue and commission are finally reconciled, including later payments for the three covered service months. Any security suspension requires prompt notice and equivalent reporting within five business days. Withholding required records is subject to section 8.2.

7.3 Record checks. Client retains relevant verification records during the closing window and until eligible collections and commission are finally reconciled. On ten business days’ notice, a confidential independent accountant may check relevant source, contract, invoice and payment records, normally once annually and additionally where reasonable evidence shows a material discrepancy. Unrelated information may be redacted. Provider pays unless the shortfall exceeds 5% of commission due for the checked period; then Client pays reasonable documented check costs and the shortfall.

These verification rights end once the closing window has ended and eligible collections and commission are reconciled, except for a specific unresolved discrepancy raised before that date, for which the relevant records remain available until resolved. No additional 24-month contractual access or checking period applies. Statutory record-retention duties remain unaffected.

7.4 No avoidance. Client must not intentionally defer deals/payments, omit records, misdescribe revenue or route attributed business through another entity to avoid commission. Equivalent commission remains due on eligible revenue collected that way. Genuine commercial delays and refunds are respected; deliberate evasion of section 6 does not defeat otherwise-earned entitlement. No double recovery or automatic penalty applies.

8. Duration, termination and suspension

8.1 Ordinary termination. The initial term is 90 days. It then continues with billing every 30 days. Either party may end the service on 30 days’ written notice. The end date is 30 days after receipt of notice, or the end of the initial 90-day term if later. Notice may be given during the initial term and may specify a later end date. Service and base fees continue until that date. Any prepaid base fee covering days after the end date is refunded pro rata within 14 days, using the $1,000 fee divided by 30 for each unused day.

8.2 Breach. Either party may terminate for a material breach that remains uncured 15 days after written notice identifying it. For unpaid undisputed fees, Provider may suspend after seven days’ written notice and terminate if unpaid at the end of the 15-day cure period. Provider may suspend immediately where reasonably necessary to prevent unlawful processing, a security incident or a serious platform violation, notifying Client promptly. Provider-caused failure does not extend Client’s minimum commitment.

8.3 Settlement. Accrued fees and eligible commissions survive termination. If Client abandons the initial commitment or causes termination by an uncured material breach, the unpaid balance of the initial $3,000 base commitment remains payable, less any costs actually saved; mandatory legal rights remain unaffected. If Client terminates for Provider’s uncured material breach, unused prepaid base fees are returned pro rata and future base fees stop. No new renewal-period fees are charged after the effective end date.

Responsibilities and safeguards

9. Future price or scope changes

Either party may propose revised pricing or scope from day 60, with at least 30 days’ written notice and an effective date at a service-period boundary no earlier than day 90. Changes require express written acceptance by both parties; silence and continued payment at the old rate are not acceptance. Without agreement, existing terms continue unless either party gives valid termination notice under 8.1.

A new commission rate applies only to prospects first attributed on or after its effective date. Earlier attributed prospects, including their eligible later collections and section 6 claims, keep the original rate and revenue limits unless both parties expressly agree otherwise.

10. Delivery responsibilities and platform limits

Provider performs with reasonable care and skill. Client supplies lawful account access, accurate source material, substantiated claims, approvals and sales feedback. Client handles sales calls, quotations, closing and customer delivery; Provider has no authority to bind Client to a customer contract.

Client has three business days to review proposed posts and supply approvals or consolidated feedback. Publishing dates allow for that review period. The parties may approve an ongoing publishing brief; otherwise silence is not approval to publish a new factual claim. Client-caused delays move dependent deadlines and do not create an unlimited backlog or extra work.

Provider may refuse unlawful instructions or activity reasonably likely to compromise accounts. Platform availability, reach, restrictions and policy changes are outside either party’s control. Provider promptly reports material issues and proposes adjustments. Platform issues do not excuse Provider’s own breach. If the core service is prevented for more than 15 consecutive days by an external event neither party caused, either may terminate and unused prepaid base fees are returned pro rata.

11. Results and liability

No number of calls, clients, followers or amount of revenue is guaranteed. Client makes its own commercial decisions. Subject to mandatory law, Provider’s total aggregate liability connected with this agreement is limited to fees actually paid to Provider under it, and Provider is not liable for indirect or consequential loss, lost profits, lost revenue or lost opportunity.

These exclusions and the cap do not apply to fraud, wilful misconduct, gross negligence, death or personal injury where liability cannot be excluded, or any other non-excludable liability. Nothing removes Client’s obligation to pay fees and commissions properly due. Client is responsible for the legality and rights in materials and claims it supplies; Provider remains responsible for its own conduct.

12. Ownership and confidentiality

Client keeps its account, audience, contacts and lead list. Rights in bespoke final content and resources transfer to Client when the fees for those deliverables are paid, subject to third-party rights. Provider retains pre-existing and reusable templates, methods, software, workflows, prompts and know-how. Client receives a perpetual, non-exclusive right to use any Provider material embedded in paid final deliverables as needed to use those deliverables. Source code, internal tools, automation infrastructure and continuing hosting/support are not transferred unless expressly agreed.

Each party protects the other’s confidential information, uses it only to fulfil this agreement, and restricts access to people bound to confidentiality who need it. Public, independently developed or lawfully obtained information is excluded. Legally required disclosure is permitted with prior notice where lawful. The obligation lasts three years after termination, and for trade secrets while protected by law.

Within ten business days after termination, Provider supplies a reasonable export of Client-owned work and records, ends operational account access (except the limited verification access under section 7) and cooperates with a basic handover. Additional migration or training requires a separate fee. Personal data is handled under section 14; access to Client’s own data is not conditional on payment of a disputed fee.

Law and data handling

13. Law, notices and disputes

Italian law governs this agreement. The courts of Latina, Italy have exclusive jurisdiction, subject to mandatory law. Before proceedings, either party sends a concise written dispute notice and the parties try in good faith to resolve it for 15 days. Urgent protective relief and mandatory procedures remain available.

Notices go to the email addresses on page 1, or a replacement notified in writing. An email is received the next business day if no delivery failure is reported; a failed delivery must be resent by another effective written method. This agreement is the entire agreement for this service and prevails over proposal summaries. Changes require written agreement; electronic signatures and counterparts may be used. Invalid provisions are severed only to the necessary extent. Neither party may transfer its obligations without the other’s written consent.

14. Personal data processing

For Client’s prospect and contact data, Client is controller and Provider is processor under applicable EU/UK data protection law. Processing covers business contact details, public professional information, messages, lead-source records and booking/sales notes of prospects, customers and Client personnel, for research, outreach, content operations, qualification and reporting during the service and handover. Sensitive personal data is not intended. Client determines lawful purposes and instructions and supplies necessary notices and legal basis.

Provider processes only on documented lawful instructions, including transfers, and alerts Client to an instruction it believes unlawful. Provider ensures confidentiality, appropriate access controls, MFA where available, secure transmission/storage, proportionate testing, and prompt breach notification without undue delay. Provider reasonably assists with rights requests, security, impact assessments and regulator consultation, supplies compliance information and permits proportionate audits. Assistance caused by Provider’s breach is at Provider’s cost; other substantial assistance may be reasonably charged as agreed without preventing legally required cooperation.

Client gives general authorisation for subprocessors under equivalent written obligations. Before using them, Provider supplies their identity, purpose and processing locations; intended additions or replacements receive at least 14 days’ notice for reasonable data-protection objections. Unresolved objections must be resolved before affected data is transferred. Provider remains responsible for subprocessor obligations and uses legally required safeguards for international transfers. No undisclosed processing vendor is authorised by this paragraph alone.

At Client’s choice, Provider returns or deletes processed data within 30 days after termination and deletes remaining processor copies, except legally required retention; backups are isolated from use and removed on the ordinary deletion cycle within 90 days. Each party separately acts as controller for necessary contract, accounting and proportionate commission-evidence records it lawfully retains. Section 7 verification access and retained evidence are limited to what is necessary for commission accounting or legal claims, under each party’s own applicable controller obligations and lawful basis. They do not authorise continued outreach or retention of unrelated CRM data. End access and delete evidence when no longer needed, subject to lawful retention duties. Section 14 prevails for processor duties.
