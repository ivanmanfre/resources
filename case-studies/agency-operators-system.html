01 · The honest read

You'd post if the math worked. It doesn't, yet.

I've audited workflows in dozens of $1–10M service businesses. The pattern is the same every time. The founder knows LinkedIn would build the brand. They have the opinions. They just don't have ninety minutes a day to draft, edit, find the hook, schedule, and write the lead magnet that follows.

1
Drafting is the bottleneck
Each post takes 45–90 minutes. Five posts a week is a part-time job nobody on your team wants and you can't afford to hire for.
2
Lead magnets die in week two
Most never ship. By day four you remember you have an agency to run. The PDF sits 80% done in Notion forever.
3
Your calls have the content
Every discovery call surfaces three posts worth of insight. Nobody has time to listen back, transcribe, and turn them into drafts.
4
Templates aren't the fix
Notion-template content systems push the work back onto you. The system has to draft in your voice, not give you a calendar to fill out.

08 · FAQ

What people ask on every discovery call.

Will it actually sound like me?

Yes, once it has enough of your voice to learn from. The profile trains off your LinkedIn archive, your call transcripts, and a short brand interview in week one. After 10–15 approved posts the edit time drops to seconds per draft.

What do you need from me to build it?

A 30-minute brand interview in week one. Access to your LinkedIn archive. Your call recordings if you have them (Fathom, Granola, whatever). Approval rights on the accounts where the system lives. That's the list.

Can I edit drafts before they ship?

Every one. The queue shows the draft, the ten hook variations, the source material, and the QA score. Edit, reject, swap hooks, change publish time. Nothing ships without your tap.

Am I locked in after the build?

No. The system runs in your accounts on your stack. Database, workspace, LinkedIn integration, landing pages, all in your name. Optional care plan if you want me to monitor and tune. Most clients keep me on it. A few run it themselves from day 15. Your call.

What if the output isn't good enough?

Weekly reviews during the build catch direction problems early. If at the end of the engagement the system isn't producing usable output, I refund the final invoice. Hasn't happened yet.
