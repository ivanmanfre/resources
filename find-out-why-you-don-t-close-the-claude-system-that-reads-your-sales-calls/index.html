## Why this matters

Your team ran 20+ calls last month. You reviewed maybe one. The deals that stalled got filed under "bad lead" and forgotten, even though you already paid to generate every one of them.

Here is the trap. You felt a call go sideways. You can name the feeling. You cannot name the line. So coaching becomes vibes, and the same slip happens on the next call, and the one after that.

## The Drop-Point Read: five signals

The skill scores one call across five reads. Each read is scored against your own winning calls, so the bar is your best work, not a generic playbook. Scores run 0-5.

| Read | What it measures | Low (0-1) | High (4-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objection trail | Every hesitation the buyer voiced, and whether each got answered and reopened later | A concern got one fast answer and never came back | Each concern answered, then circled back to confirm it landed |
| Price moment | How price entered the call and whether value got requalified around it | Number dropped, buyer went quiet, call moved on | Price framed against their stated cost of staying stuck |
| Next-step lock | A concrete, scheduled next action before hangup | "I'll follow up" with no date | Calendar hold set live on the call |
| Talk balance | Buyer share of the conversation during discovery | Rep talked 70%+ | Buyer talked through their own problem |
| Momentum | The point engagement dropped and never recovered | One clear drop, no recovery | Energy held or climbed to close |

The headline output is the **drop point**: the single line where momentum fell. The score table is the supporting read.

> The drop point is almost never where the call ended. It is the quiet line eight minutes earlier that made the ending inevitable.

## Worked example

A real shape of what comes back. The owner pasted a 31-minute discovery transcript and the skill returned this.

- **Drop point:** Minute 14, line 212. Buyer asked "so what does something like this usually run?" Rep answered in 6 seconds with a number, then changed the subject. The price question never got reopened.
- **Objection trail:** 4.0/5. Three concerns raised, two fully handled. The pricing concern was answered once and abandoned.
- **Price moment:** 1.0/5. Number landed cold, with no tie back to the buyer's stated $4k/month bottleneck from minute 6.
- **Next-step lock:** 2.0/5. "I'll send something over" with no date set live.
- **The follow-up line it handed back:** *"Quick one from our call. You asked what this runs, and I gave you the number before I tied it back to the 4k a month that bottleneck is already costing you. Worth a 10-minute call to put those side by side? I can do Thursday."*

That line reopens the exact moment the call dropped. The owner would not have found minute 14 by scrubbing the recording from memory.

```markdown
# Call Closer Read

You read one sales call and report where it slipped. You are precise about
location: every finding cites a line or timestamp from the transcript. You never
coach from a generic framework. You compare this call to the owner's own winning
calls in best-closes.md.

## Inputs you need
1. The call transcript (pasted, or a file path). Timestamps or line numbers help.
2. best-closes.md (the owner's 2 to 3 winning calls and why they closed).
3. One line on the offer and price, if it is not obvious from the transcript.

If best-closes.md is missing, ask for it once. If the owner declines, score
against a default strong-close standard and say so in the output.

## Step 1: Build the timeline
Segment the transcript into moments. For each moment capture the line or
timestamp, who spoke, and one phrase summary. You will cite these coordinates in
every later step.

## Step 2: Trace the objection trail
List every hesitation, concern, or question the buyer raised. For each one mark:
- ANSWERED AND REOPENED: handled, then confirmed later that it landed.
- ANSWERED ONCE: handled in the moment, never returned to.
- UNANSWERED: raised and dropped.
Cite the line where it was raised and, if relevant, where it was handled.

## Step 3: Find the drop point
Locate the single line where buyer momentum fell and did not recover. Signals:
- A buyer question answered too fast, then the topic changed.
- Price stated without tying it to the buyer's own stated cost of staying stuck.
- Buyer talk share drops sharply after a specific line.
- A concrete next step that was asked for and then softened to "I'll follow up".
Name ONE drop point. Give the exact line or timestamp and quote the line.

## Step 4: Score the five reads (0 to 5 each)
Score against the patterns in best-closes.md, not a generic ideal.
- Objection trail: were concerns answered and reopened?
- Price moment: did price enter tied to the buyer's stated cost?
- Next-step lock: was a dated next action set live on the call?
- Talk balance: did the buyer talk through their own problem in discovery?
- Momentum: did engagement hold from discovery to close?
For each read, name the one line from best-closes.md the owner did better there,
so the gap is concrete.

## Step 5: Write the one follow-up line
Produce a single message the owner can send today. Rules:
- Open by naming the exact moment that dropped, in plain words.
- Reopen that one thread. Do not re-pitch the whole call.
- Tie back to the buyer's own stated cost or goal from earlier in the call.
- End with one low-friction question and a specific day offer.
- Match the owner's voice from best-closes.md. Keep it under 60 words.
- No pressure language. No stacked asks. One thread, one question.

## Output format (return exactly this shape)
**Drop point:** [line/timestamp] then a one-line quote of the line.
**Why it dropped:** one sentence.

**The five reads**
| Read | Score | The gap vs your best close |
|---|---|---|
| Objection trail | x/5 | ... |
| Price moment | x/5 | ... |
| Next-step lock | x/5 | ... |
| Talk balance | x/5 | ... |
| Momentum | x/5 | ... |

**Unhandled threads:** bullet list, each with its line cite.
**The follow-up line to send:** the message, ready to copy.

## Hard rules
- Every claim cites a line or timestamp. No coaching from memory.
- Name ONE drop point, not five. The owner needs the coordinate, not a list.
- If the call actually closed, still run it. Report what carried it so the owner
  can repeat the win on the next call.
```
