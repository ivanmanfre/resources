# 18 real LinkedIn openings

A Content Brain draft kit by Ivan Manfredi. 19 October 2026.

## Start with a draft you already have

Choose a source example that fits the job of your post. Use your facts for the opening and check that the body delivers it. The examples below belong to their named authors.

## A completed rewrite

Illustrative demo, also used in the public Content Brain guide. It is not a client result or a claim of work Ivan performed.

Original draft:

Daily sales reporting is easy to automate with n8n. My workflow pulls orders, groups sales by product and emails me the summary.

Complete opening options for this demo:

Show the build: I built a daily sales report that lands in my inbox.

Name the tool: My n8n workflow turns orders into a daily sales summary.

Show the output: I can see sales by product in one email.

The full rewrite uses the build opening. The other two are editorial options using the same supplied facts. These demo openings have no measured performance result.

Rewritten draft:

I built a daily sales report that lands in my inbox.

It runs in n8n:
→ Pull orders
→ Group sales by product
→ Email the daily summary

Now I can see what sold in one email.

The opening brings the supplied build into view. The body keeps the same operations. The rewrite adds no time saving, client, revenue or performance claim.

In the October 9 AI/automation cohort, openings about what the author did or saw were associated with +15.0% adjusted geometric engagement (95% interval 9.1 to 21.1; 4,867 posts, 69 authors). The direction replicated in later posts. That is historical support for considering this opening, with no measured result for this demo.

## What happened in work posts

The work-only check retained 828 selected same-author, same-format pairs after excluding personal, celebration, life-arc and promotional posts. For each row, only pairs where one post had the feature count. A win means stronger historical engagement under the study measure. Each row has its own denominator; features overlap.

| Opening feature | Won | 95% interval | Comparable pairs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Humour | 63.3% | 56 to 70% | 158 |
| Familiar name | 60.4% | 54 to 66% | 268 |
| Specialist topic | 58.0% | 52 to 64% | 231 |
| Concrete detail | 55.6% | 49 to 61% | 261 |
| List or how-to promise | 55.5% | 49 to 62% | 220 |
| Unexpected claim | 54.5% | 49 to 60% | 363 |
| Author's own action or moment | 52.9% | 47 to 59% | 263 |
| Confident verdict | 52.4% | 47 to 57% | 368 |
| Warm mention of people | 52.2% | 38 to 66% | 46 |
| Calls out the reader | 50.2% | 44 to 56% | 273 |

These are exploratory subgroup findings with multiple comparisons. Humour, familiar names and specialist topics are useful options to examine. An author-moment opening at 52.9%, with an interval of 47 to 59%, showed no clear edge in this subset. Keep the form your actual material supports.

## How the research works

The October 16 study used 17,322 public posts from 217 authors across four research cohorts. It selected 1,709 high/low pairs from the same author and format; high posts had at least 2 times the author baseline and low posts at most 0.7 times. The work-only analysis retained 828 pairs. Previews were the first three lines, capped at 210 characters, with media hidden.

The engagement measure was log(1 + likes + 3 × reposts), adjusted for age and compared with the author's same-format median near that date. It measures observed engagement. It does not measure see-more clicks, leads or a new draft's chance of success. Topic and image effects can remain in the comparison.

The October 9 writing study separately used 10,629 posts from 140 authors across three niches. Those samples overlap with the October 16 study. Their counts are kept separate.

The toolkit's rewrite and choice of examples are editorial work. This kit adds no prediction model or automatic publishing capability.
